My criteria were less consistent than I assumed

  • "About 31 December" was first too approximate for me; I accepted it when the answer cites the term, because I can check the date at once.
  • A wrong fact and a missing fact are not the same.
  • For "when does it end?" on a contract that renews automatically, a complete answer also gives the last day to give notice.
  • Naming other vendors, or quoting their terms, fails. Exception: the user asks for a comparison (negotiation, usual practice).
  • Q3: I expected one price; an answer with both prices (before and now) is better, because I can compare.
  • Drafted messages: a small invention passes when it does not block the owner (golden 4); a doubt about a date that comes from the code fails (golden 6).

What the echo row says

Echo returns the question as the answer, and still scores 0.846 on average: a question has no wrong date, names no other vendor and claims nothing is missing. These scorers catch a bad answer, not a missing one. That is why no single one of them can be the headline, and why the judge exists.

03 The judge

Separation, agreement, and whether I used it

One judge, for one property: does the answer say something is missing that our files have?

It scores by meaning, so it is not tied to the 5 phrases the missing_document scorer looks for. Its reference is what our files hold per vendor (the document list and key facts: term, renewal, notice, fees), no contract text. Answers by anthropic/claude-sonnet-5-5, judged by a different model, anthropic/claude-opus-5-5.

TestKnown-goodKnown-bad

0.00 / 1.00separation: echo / oracle
20 / 20agreement with my hand scores = 1.00; chance floor 0.75 (always answering 1). 5 of the 20 are 0

The line the course asks for

Separation 0.00 / 1.00 (echo / oracle), agreement 1.00 on 20 (floor 0.75): used.

What the first run taught me

The judge failed the oracle on Q12 (Do Vendor05 and Vendor10 have a non-compete clause?). The expected answer is a clause inside a contract, not a document. But the oracle's decline text "Missing document." claimed a document was missing while we hold every document for both vendors. I changed it to "Not in the documents." and re-ran: oracle 0.92, then 1.00.

20 of 20 is an upper bound: the rubric's example of a 0 is my row 1, and I labelled the rows after reading the same traces. Section 04 tests the judge on answers it has not seen.

The rubric (JUDGE_RUBRIC in harness.py) 
You check one answer about our vendor contracts for one failure: the answer says
something is missing, not found or not stated, but our files have it.

Score 0 if the answer says a document or a key fact is missing, not found or not
stated, and "What our files hold" above shows we have it. The search results are
not our files: "it wasn't in the search results" is a missing claim too.

Score 0 also if the text is not an answer to the question, for example it only
repeats the question or says the turn limit was reached.

Score 1 otherwise. [...] Wrong dates, other vendors, length and style do not matter here.

Reply with JSON only: {"score": 0 or 1, "why": "<the false missing claim, or none>"}

Shortened: the examples of a 0 and a 1 and three edge cases are in the file.

No judge for the drafted messages: 1 failure in 9 traces (rank 4), low impact, and message type and act_by are code, checked by check_goldens.py.

08 Decision

The eval row: decision, rejected, why, what would change my mind

Headline: the judge for false missing claims, 0.692, 95% CI [0.462, 0.923], n = 13

Decision, in short

The judge covers the top failure (7 of 17), and it caught both plausible versions the free phrase check missed. The four free scorers run beside it.

What I rejected, in short

missing_document as headline: free, but caught 2 of 4 generated rank-1 failures, the judge 4 of 4.

A judge for the drafted messages: 1 failure in 9.

A stricter dates rule: it failed a right date projection.

Step 5 scenarios: the scored endpoint answers one question.

What would change my mind, in short

A second run flips judge scores, or the judge model changes: then missing_document becomes the headline.

Judge agreement below the floor on new hand scores.

Real traces at work showing a different top failure.

Decision: Headline: missing_claim_judge (failures.md rank 1, false missing claims) 0.692, 95% CI [0.462, 0.923], on 13 contract questions; 4 model-free scorers run beside it

His answers to the three questions, in my words

1. Would you use the answers, and how? How many good answers out of 10 to stop checking?

He trusts an answer that says it found something. He checks every "not found" ("by default, zero trust"), and whether the list of documents behind the answer is complete. He would accept 1 wrong answer in 10: then he would trust it without checking.

2. Are these the questions owners ask, would the failures hurt, what test would convince you?

He did not name a test. Going through the 13 questions: realistic, but the security question is asked wrong. He would ask whether the vendor is certified (SOC 2 Type 2 or ISO 27001, and for which perimeter) and for its public security rating, and wants both as fields in the database.

3. Thinking of your last renewal, what did you need that you did not see today?

"At this stage, I would say no." Quite complete on reading the contracts for a version 1. Comparing products and tools across contracts comes later.

Also agreed: complexity = who uses the product + its features, instances and add-ons; the "new review cycle" sentence is removed from goldens 5 and 6.

Demo timed alone before the Loom: [my time] for 5:00. Other points he raised, for later weeks (duplicate tools across contracts, licences ordered vs used, document version history, EU processing for the model route) are in stakeholders.md.
