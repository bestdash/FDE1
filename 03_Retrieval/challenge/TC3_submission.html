Week 3 of the project, told as it happened

The search found the words.
It lost the contract.

I'm building an agent that helps IT contract owners renew or stop software contracts on time. This week: teaching it to read the contracts. 10 questions labelled before any code, 3 ways to search measured, 1 endpoint that names its sources.

The need. Owners ask contract questions in their own words: when can we stop it, what did we pay. The answer sits in one section of one of 19 documents.
The trick. I cut every document into its sections and let the model search them with BM25: word matching, the simplest search there is.
The surprise. First run:7 of 10Only the Parties section names the vendor; every other section says "the Vendor". Asked for a notice period, it answered 60 days. The order form said 90.
The fix. I added the contract's identity (file, vendor, document title) to every section: 10 of 10, on two runs, with half the text read.

The search did not fail on words.
It failed on knowing which contract a paragraph belongs to.

An alert built on that 60-day answer would have been 30 days late. This page follows the path: the questions, the measurements, the endpoint, and the decision.

My labelling notes

  • Top-k floor: 3 of the 10 questions need two documents (an order form that overrides the main terms, a renewal order that changes them, last year's price against this year's). So top-1 cannot pass them: k is at least 2.
  • 2 declines: renewal terms that live in online terms not attached (SYN-03), and a security-incident clause that no document has.
  • Sources: my own questions as a contract owner; 4 also match a column of the sponsor's tracking sheet. Questions name the vendor, as owners do.
  • Left out on purpose: "which contracts should I renew in the next 3 months?" is a query over all contract records plus today's date, not a search: it belongs to the alert stage, answered from the Stage 1 records.

02 Schema

The schema, with sources

Every field the agent keeps, and every answer it gives, carries the section it came from

A schema is the exact format the agent accepts and returns. This week adds a contract record in front of the drafting step built in TC1, and a small answer format for the question service. The data moves like this:

Read the contractTC3 · first part built
→
Save the record10 fields with sources · not built yet
→
Alert the ownerWeek 4 or 5
→
Decide and draftTC1 · built: takes the end date, the last day to say no and the file status

The contract record (the charter calls it Stage 1)

In: a contract, for example SYN-03
Out: 10 fields, each with its source (document and section) or "not found"

start_date term_months end_date renewal_type notice_days annual_contract_value total_contract_value pricing_model price_per_user features

Computed by code, never by the model: the end date when not stated, the missing contract value, the last day to say no.

What the endpoint (the app's question service) returns today

answerthe answer text, with [file#section] after each claim
sourcesthe sections the answer used, checked against the real sections
unknown_citationscitations that match no section: shown as a warning, never dropped

The schema decision, as written in my decision log

Decision: Stage 1 has its own schema: In contract_id; Out 10 fields, each with its source or "not found" (see CHARTER.md). Step B's Request/Response stay as they are. The question endpoint has its own small answer model, ContractAnswer (answer, sources, unknown_citations)

What I rejected: Contract fields inside step B's Request; one field per feature or price line; price per user for every contract; a users count parsed from the text; the model computing dates

Why: Features differ per vendor (free user tiers, SSO, security and support add-ons, AI credits), so one field per feature would make the schema grow with every vendor. A features list keeps one shape for all vendors; evals count how many expected features are found. price_per_user is filled only when the contract states a price per user, and it is the comparison metric across years and vendors. No users count: a number of users in a contract may not mean our staff (one counted people we train), so it stays as text in features. Dates and the missing contract value are computed by code. A silent contract gives renewal_type "not found": the system does not guess. In = contract_id, so the retrieval is finding the right section across a contract's documents; grouping real files into contracts is a production step. sources lives in ContractAnswer, not in step B's Response: sources belong to the answer that retrieved them, and step B retrieves nothing. The endpoint takes a free question, not contract_id: people ask in their own words, and finding the right contract is part of the test. Stage 1's 10-field extraction reuses the same retrieval later

What would change my mind: Owners need to compare one feature across vendors (for example, what SSO costs): then that feature gets its own field, as price_per_user already is for user-based contracts

Stage 1 = step 1 in the Final product (read the contract and save its record) · step B = the TC1 drafting step (step 3) · evals = automatic scoring against labelled answers.

What the numbers say

The most important fix is Q1. rag-v1 answered 60 days with confidence, but the order form overrides the agreement to 90 days, and the override section was never retrieved (found by the search). An alert built on that answer would be 30 days late.

Declines (questions it should say no to) are the most expensive answer. Measured in rag-v2b, Q10 used 39% of the run's input tokens (text sent to the model) and 33% of its cost: every turn (round) sends all earlier evidence again. DCI (read whole pages) declined in 4 calls for $0.033, against 6 calls for $0.081.

Dense (search by meaning) is weak on exact names: for "cancellation period Vendor04" its top hit was the empty title chunk (a section with only a title) and its third hit another vendor. Its decline question hit the turn limit.

Same code, different scores: an earlier run of the same baseline gave 5/10. Cost moved 15 to 20% between two runs of rag-v2 even at temperature 0 (the model's most repeatable setting). Only rag-v2 has two runs.

The docstring (the instructions an AI agent will read to use this service) 
@app.api(name="answer_contract_question")
def answer_contract_question(question: str) -> str:
    """Answer one question about our contracts, asked in plain words.
    Searches the contract documents and answers only from what it finds.
    Returns the answer with [file#section] citations inline, then a last line
    "sources: <file#section>, ..." or "sources: none".
    A "warning:" line lists citations that match no document section.
    If the contracts do not cover the question, it says so, names the document
    that seems missing, and suggests asking the vendor for the last signed copy.
    Dates computed from stated terms (for example start date plus term) come
    with the terms used: check them before acting on a deadline.
    Read-only: sends nothing, writes nothing, never guesses a figure."""

What it does on the decline question

On the decline question (Q10): two behaviours seen. In its first run the endpoint hit the 6-turn limit and returned the fallback (no answer, no sources): the turn limit stopped the model before it could follow the decline instruction. The fallback now says it found no answer, that the topic may not be covered or a document may be missing, and to ask the vendor. In endpoint-v1 it gave an explained decline: what the contracts do say about security, no invented figure, and a suggestion to ask the vendor about the security add-on.

Q10 = the security question no document answers · 6-turn limit = at most 6 search rounds · fallback = the standard "no answer found" message.

The 10 questions through the endpoint

All gold sections cited on 8 of 9 answerable questions. No invented citation. Q2 missed the master agreement and told the user to ask the vendor for it, although it is in the corpus (the 19 documents): a quiet failure, because the advice sounds reasonable.

Runs where the app runs

Locally and in Docker, with the contracts mounted read-only (the container can read them, never change them, and they are never copied into the image).
A date that was one day late

The model computes deadlines from the stated terms and labels them as its own calculation. For 90 days before 30 June 2028 it once wrote "roughly 2 April 2028". The right date is 1 April: a notice sent on 2 April is only 89 days before the end, so too late, and the contract renews for another 24 months. In the next run it gave 1 April.

The charter already says code computes dates. I kept the model's calculation for TC3 so the endpoint is the setup I measured, and a code date tool comes in Week 4, measured as its own run.

05 Decision

The retrieval row: decision, rejected, why, what would change my mind

Ship BM25 with the contract's identity in every chunk

The course asks for one decision row: what I chose, what I rejected, why, and what would make me switch. The last column is what turns a preference into an engineering position.

Decision, in short

Keyword search over each document's sections, with the contract's name and vendor on every section. Same accuracy as reading whole pages, cheaper, and no model to approve for the search.

What I rejected, in short

DCI (read whole pages): the same 10/10, but it read 50% more text and cost more.

Dense (search by meaning, run locally): 9/10, weak on exact vendor names. Its $0.23 is the answer model reading the found sections and writing the answer; the local embeddings cost nothing.

What would change my mind, in short

DCI if declines go above about 20% of questions.

Dense or hybrid (keyword plus meaning search) if owners often ask by product name instead of vendor name.

No identity prefix (the contract's name on every section) if the vendor-to-contract mapping cannot come from the contract record.

The row, as written in my decision log

Decision: Retrieval: BM25 over the ## sections of each document, with the contract's identity (file, vendor, document title) added to every chunk, in a tool loop (rag-v2). The endpoint answer_contract_question ships exactly this setup (k 4, max 8 per search, 6 turns)

What I rejected: DCI (index, grep, read page): 10/10 and 9/9 cited, but 127,172 evidence chars and $0.29 for the 10 questions, against 84,846 and $0.25. Dense (bge-small-en-v1.5, local): 9/10 and 8/9, $0.23, weak on exact vendor names, and its decline question hit the turn limit

Why: Small corpus (19 documents, 173 sections), so no vector database. The questions name the vendor and exact contract terms, where word matching is strong. Every answer names its file and section: Stage 1 extraction needs a source for every field, and Week 4 needs to know which passage caused a wrong answer. BM25 is word counting in code, so the retrieval step needs no model and no model approval; dense needs an embedding model, which would go through the same vendor review as any model (Week 2 model row). The fix that mattered: a section cut out of a contract loses which contract it belongs to; the identity prefix took rag-v1 from 7/10 to 10/10 (calls 60 to 34, evidence halved), on 2 runs. Known limits: one run per technique except rag-v2, and the same baseline code gave 5/10 and 7/10 on two runs; in the endpoint run, Q2 missed the master agreement and told the user to ask the vendor for a document already in the corpus; the model still computes dates (a notice deadline one day late in 1 of 2 runs), moving to code in Week 4

What would change my mind: Switch to DCI if declines go above about 20% of questions: it declined in 4 calls for $0.033, against 6 calls for $0.081. 20% is my starting threshold to measure, not an industry standard. Add dense or hybrid if owners often ask by product name instead of vendor name, which word matching on the vendor misses: Step 6 is the first check. Drop the identity prefix if the vendor-to-contract mapping cannot come from the contract record (here it comes from the synthetic file name)

Tool loop = the model searches, reads and decides whether to search again · vector database = a database built for meaning search · embedding model = turns text into numbers · Stage 1 extraction = step 1 in the Final product.

06 Non-text content

The four non-text answers

Real contracts are text; scans are rare, and recordings are not the ground truth

Contracts are not always clean text. The course asks four questions about scans, images and recordings, even when the answer is "there are almost none".

  1. Visual, or text as pixels? Text. The contracts are mostly vendor PDFs with real text; scanned paper is rare and almost gone, and new contracts arrive as e-signature PDFs. A scan, when there is one, is text rendered as pixels.
  2. A number only in the pixels? Rarely. Price tables come as text. The usual image is a scanned signature page, and an unsigned text version of the same contract usually exists, so the terms are read from the text and the scan only shows the signature.
  3. Descriptions, or one parse per image? No descriptions today. Any scan found (most likely an old contract) gets OCR once, in a batch job on the pilot VM inside the network, and the extracted terms are checked against the text version when there is one.
  4. Video or audio? Recordings of vendor calls can exist. A transcript does not answer the question: people discuss terms they did not agree to, so the signed contract stays the ground truth.

OCR = text recognition on a scanned image · batch job = run once for all files · pilot VM = our test server inside the company network · ground truth = the reference.
